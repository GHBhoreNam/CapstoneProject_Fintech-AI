README Note: Fairness, Proxy Risk, and Governance Considerations

Although this dataset does not contain explicit protected attributes such as gender, religion, ethnicity, or location, several available features could act as proxy variables for protected characteristics in a real-world deployment. For example, monthly_income_inr may reflect broader socioeconomic patterns that are correlated with demographic groups. Similarly, employment_type (salaried, self-employed, gig) may indirectly capture labor-market segmentation that varies across populations. Even credit_bureau_score, while highly predictive of credit risk, can encode historical inequalities in access to formal financial services. As a result, a model could unintentionally produce disparate outcomes across protected groups despite never directly observing those attributes.

The handling of missing bureau scores deserves particular attention. In this project, missing scores were preserved through an is_thin_file indicator and subsequently imputed using the training-set median. While this approach is technically sound and avoids data leakage, thin-file applicants often represent individuals who are new to formal credit systems. In a production environment, these applicants may include populations that are historically underserved by traditional lending channels. Automatically declining such applicants could therefore create fairness and inclusion concerns.

Before deployment, a key governance recommendation is a maker-checker (human-in-the-loop) review process for applicants who are declined or receive unusually adverse pricing while also being flagged as thin-file (is_thin_file = 1). Under this control, the model provides a recommendation, but a trained credit officer reviews borderline or high-impact decisions before final approval or rejection. This allows consideration of additional evidence such as bank transaction behavior, income verification, or other alternate-data signals that may not be fully captured by the current model.

In addition, periodic fairness monitoring should be performed after deployment. The organization should evaluate approval rates, pricing outcomes, and default performance across legally permitted customer segments, investigate any unexplained disparities, and retrain or recalibrate the model when necessary. Combining statistical monitoring with human oversight provides a stronger governance framework than relying solely on automated decision-making.
